# Meaning-first translation

Translate to keep the author's intent, not a dictionary gloss.

## By content type

- **Poetry** — Preserve image, rhythm cues, and emotional charge. Prefer
  natural lines in the target language over mirror syntax.
- **Narrative / story** — Keep voice, pacing, and character perspective.
  Do not flatten idioms into literal calques.
- **Case study** — Keep problem → approach → outcome structure. Preserve
  metrics, product names, and client names exactly.
- **Paper / research** — Keep claims, hedges, and terminology precise.
  Do not overclaim. Leave citations, DOIs, and proper nouns intact.
- **Portfolio copy** — Keep confident, concise marketing tone without
  inventing achievements.

## Always

1. Detect register (formal / casual / literary / technical) before wording.
2. Preserve proper nouns, brands, and code-like tokens.
3. Prefer clarity in the target language over one-to-one word order.
4. If a phrase has no clean equivalent, paraphrase the meaning and note
   the choice in `fidelityNotes` on `submit_replacements`.
